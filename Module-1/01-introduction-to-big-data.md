# 1. Introduction to Big Data

## What is data?
Data is a collection of raw facts, figures, observations, or measurements that can be processed to obtain meaningful information.

**Example:** Individual student marks are data; calculating their average produces information.

## What is Big Data?
Big Data refers to large, complex, and rapidly generated datasets that may be difficult to store, process, and analyse efficiently using traditional tools alone. Specialised technologies can distribute storage and processing across multiple machines.

### Examples
- Social media posts, photos, videos, comments, and messages
- Search-engine queries and logs
- Stock-market transactions
- Online shopping records and clickstream activity
- Sensor and IoT data

## Why is Big Data important?
1. Supports data-driven decision-making.
2. Enables predictive analytics.
3. Helps personalise recommendations.
4. Supports fraud detection.
5. Helps improve public-health planning.
6. Can improve operational efficiency and reduce costs.
7. Helps organisations understand customer behaviour.

## Benefits and examples

| Benefit | Example |
|---|---|
| Better decisions | Retailers analyse buying patterns |
| Operational efficiency | Predictive maintenance in manufacturing |
| Cost reduction | Optimising energy distribution |
| Innovation | Analysing clinical-trial data |
| Competitive advantage | Improving delivery routes |
| Market understanding | Analysing reviews and social posts |
| Healthcare improvement | Forecasting patient admissions |
| Real-time analytics | Updating stock availability |

## Data mining
**Definition:** Data mining is the process of discovering useful patterns, trends, and relationships in datasets.

**Example:** Finding that customers who buy bread often buy butter.

### Common applications
- Customer segmentation
- Fraud detection
- Market-basket analysis
- Recommendation systems
- Sales forecasting

## Big Data vs. data mining
- **Big Data** concerns large and complex datasets and the technologies used to store and process them.
- **Data mining** focuses on discovering patterns and relationships in data.
