# 1. Big Data Fundamentals

## Q1. What is Big Data?

**Answer:** Big Data refers to very large, complex, and rapidly growing
collections of data that are difficult to store, manage, process, and
analyze efficiently using traditional tools such as spreadsheets or
conventional relational databases alone. Special technologies are used
to store and analyze it.

## Q2. Give examples of Big Data.

**Answer:** Examples include social-media posts, photos and videos;
online shopping and payment transactions; sensor data from machines;
search-engine logs; stock-market trades; GPS data; and hospital records.

## Q3. Give examples mentioned in the notes.

**Answer:** - **New York Stock Exchange:** the notes give an example of
about one terabyte of new trade data per day. - **Facebook/social
media:** the notes describe very large daily volumes generated by
photos, videos, messages, and comments. - **Google:** search queries and
user interactions generate search logs and related data.

*Viva note:* These quantities are examples from the lecture notes, not
guaranteed current statistics.

## Q5. Explain Volume.

**Answer:** Volume means the quantity of data generated and stored. Big
Data may reach terabytes, petabytes, or larger scales. For example, a
social-media platform receives enormous numbers of photos, videos,
likes, and comments.

## Q6. Explain Velocity.

**Answer:** Velocity is the speed at which data is generated,
transmitted, and sometimes analyzed. Examples include stock-market
updates, online payment events, and trending topics that need real-time
analysis.

## Q7. Explain Variety.

**Answer:** Variety refers to the different formats and sources of data.
It includes structured tables, semi-structured JSON/XML/logs, and
unstructured images, audio, video, and free text.

## Q8. Explain Veracity.

**Answer:** Veracity refers to the quality and trustworthiness of data.
Data may be incomplete, inconsistent, duplicated, biased, or inaccurate.
Data cleaning and validation help improve veracity.

## Q9. Explain Value.

**Answer:** Value means the useful outcomes extracted from data.
Collecting data alone is not enough; analysis should support better
decisions, recommendations, fraud detection, efficiency, or other
meaningful results.

## Q10. How do you distinguish the 5 Vs in a viva?

**Answer:** Ask five questions: - **Volume:** How much data? -
**Velocity:** How fast is it generated or handled? - **Variety:** What
forms does it take? - **Veracity:** How reliable is it? - **Value:**
What useful outcome can be extracted?

## Q11. Why is Big Data important?

**Answer:** It supports improved decision-making, predictive analysis,
personalized recommendations, fraud detection, public-health tracking,
operational optimization, and faster responses to changing conditions.

## Q12. List the benefits of Big Data.

**Answer:** 1. Enhanced decision-making. 2. Improved operational
efficiency. 3. Cost reduction. 4. Innovation and product development. 5.
Competitive advantage. 6. Better understanding of markets and customers.
7. Improved healthcare outcomes. 8. Real-time analytics.

## Q13. Explain predictive analysis with an example.

**Answer:** Predictive analysis uses historical and current data to
estimate likely future events. For example, a manufacturer analyzes
sensor readings to predict equipment failure and schedule maintenance
before a breakdown.

## Q14. How can Big Data improve healthcare?

**Answer:** Hospitals can analyze patient records and admission trends
to plan staffing and beds, support diagnosis, identify health trends,
and help predict disease outbreaks. Data privacy and quality must be
protected.

## Q15. How can Big Data support fraud detection?

**Answer:** A bank can analyze transaction amounts, locations, times,
devices, and historical patterns to identify unusual activity.
Suspicious transactions can be flagged for further checks.

## Q16. Explain real-time analytics.

**Answer:** Real-time analytics processes data quickly enough to support
immediate or near-immediate action. A traffic system may use current
road and GPS information to adjust traffic signals or suggest
alternative routes.

## Q17. What is the difference between data and information?

**Answer:** Data consists of raw facts, such as individual transactions.
Information is data that has been processed and organized to convey
meaning, such as total sales by day.

# 2. Types of Data

## Q18. What are the types of Big Data described in the notes?

**Answer:** Structured, unstructured, semi-structured, and hybrid data.

## Q19. What is structured data?

**Answer:** Structured data follows a predefined format or schema, often
organized into rows and columns. It is commonly stored in relational
databases and queried using SQL.

**Examples:** Employee tables, bank transactions with fixed fields,
spreadsheet reports, and customer records.

## Q20. Give an example of structured data.

**Answer:**

  Employee_ID   Name              Department     Salary
  ------------- ----------------- ------------ --------
  2365          Rajesh Kulkarni   Finance        650000
  3398          Pratibha Joshi    Admin          650000
  7465          Shushil Roy       Admin          500000

Each row is a record and each column represents a defined attribute.

## Q21. What are sources of structured data?

**Answer:** Sources include human-generated financial and sales data,
forms and surveys, and machine-generated records such as sensor
measurements when they follow a fixed schema. Relational database
management systems (RDBMS) are commonly used to manage it.

## Q22. What is unstructured data?

**Answer:** Unstructured data does not follow a rigid, predefined
tabular schema. It may contain text, images, videos, audio, photographs,
and mixed content. Its analysis often requires specialized processing.

**Examples:** CCTV recordings, photographs, satellite images, video,
social-media posts, and free-form customer reviews.

## Q23. Why is unstructured data challenging to analyze?

**Answer:** It can be large, varied, and difficult to fit into rows and
columns. It may require image processing, speech recognition,
natural-language processing, or other specialized techniques to extract
useful information.

## Q24. What is semi-structured data?

**Answer:** Semi-structured data does not use a rigid relational-table
structure but includes labels, tags, keys, or other organizational
markers. This makes it easier to interpret than completely unstructured
data in many cases.

**Examples:** JSON, XML, and many log records.

## Q25. Give an XML example of semi-structured data.

**Answer:**

``` xml
<rec>
  <name>Prashant Rao</name>
  <sex>Male</sex>
  <age>35</age>
</rec>
```

The tags identify the meaning of each value, but the data is not
necessarily stored as a conventional relational table.

## Q26. What is JSON?

**Answer:** JSON stands for JavaScript Object Notation. It is a
text-based format that represents data using key-value pairs and arrays.
It is commonly used for data exchange and web APIs.

Example:

``` json
{
  "name": "Prashant Rao",
  "age": 35,
  "sex": "Male"
}
```

## Q27. What is hybrid data?

**Answer:** Hybrid data refers to using a combination of data types,
especially structured and unstructured data, to gain a more complete
understanding. For example, an online store can combine purchase records
with written reviews and product images.

## Q29. Are JSON and XML structured or unstructured?

**Answer:** In the classification used in these notes, JSON and XML are
**semi-structured** because they contain keys or tags but do not require
a rigid relational-table structure.

## Q30. Are clickstream logs structured or semi-structured?

**Answer:** It depends on their format. Event logs stored as JSON with
variable fields are commonly treated as semi-structured. If transformed
into a fixed relational schema, they can be stored as structured data.

## Q31. How can structured and unstructured data work together?

**Answer:** Structured purchase history can identify what a customer has
bought, while unstructured reviews or images can reveal sentiment,
needs, or preferences. Combining both can improve recommendations.

## Q32. Classify the following.

-   Quiz scores in a table --- **Structured**
-   Recorded video lecture --- **Unstructured**
-   Student feedback in JSON --- **Semi-structured**
-   Bank transaction with fixed fields --- **Structured**
-   Customer call recording --- **Unstructured**
-   Web activity stored as XML --- **Semi-structured**
-   Tweets and free-text comments --- **Unstructured**
-   Campaign spreadsheet --- **Structured**
-   HTML blog page --- often treated as **semi-structured** because of
    markup tags; the extracted article text is unstructured.

## Q33. What tools or techniques can help analyze these data types?

**Answer:** Relational databases and SQL can handle structured records.
Hadoop ecosystem tools can store and process large datasets. NLP
libraries such as NLTK or spaCy can analyze text; web parsers such as
BeautifulSoup can extract content from HTML; and image/audio tools can
analyze media.

# 3. Big Data Case Studies and Data Mining

## Q34. What is data mining?

**Answer:** Data mining is the process of discovering useful patterns,
trends, relationships, or associations in large datasets.

## Q35. Give a simple example of data mining.

**Answer:** A retailer analyzes receipts and discovers that customers
who buy bread often buy butter. It can use this association to improve
product placement or recommendations.

## Q36. Why is data mining useful?

**Answer:** It helps understand customer behavior, identify patterns,
improve recommendations, support decision-making, and discover trends
that may not be obvious from individual records.

## Q37. Explain the Walmart Big Data case study.

**Answer:** The notes describe Walmart using data mining to discover
products that are bought together and improve product recommendations.
Its Big Data approach supports customer shopping experiences, e-commerce
personalization, and logistics efficiency. Hadoop and NoSQL technologies
are mentioned as part of its data infrastructure.

**Likely follow-up:** What is the main objective?\
To improve the shopping experience while increasing the efficiency of
operations and logistics.

of high demand. Machine-learning algorithms can help estimate where
demand is strong.

Volume, velocity, and value are especially relevant because Uber
processes many ride events, responds to changing demand, and uses
analysis to make pricing and allocation decisions.

**Likely follow-up:** How does Netflix personalize recommendations?\
It analyzes patterns in what users watch and how they interact with

## Q40. Explain the eBay case study.

## Q42. How can an EdTech company use different data types?

**Answer:** Structured quiz scores and attendance can identify student
performance; unstructured recorded lectures can be analyzed for
engagement; semi-structured JSON feedback can help identify course

# 4. Issues with Big Data

## Q49. List the main issues with Big Data from the notes.

**Answer:** 1. Data privacy and security. 2. Data quality. 3. Data
integration. 4. Data storage. 5. Complexity of analysis. 6. Shortage of
skilled professionals.

## Q50. Explain data privacy and security.

**Answer:** Large datasets may contain personal, financial, or
confidential information. Organizations must prevent unauthorized
access, use appropriate access controls, protect data, and comply with
relevant privacy requirements.

## Q51. What is data quality?

**Answer:** Data quality refers to whether data is accurate, complete,
consistent, timely, and reliable. Missing values, duplicates, incorrect
records, and inconsistent formats can lead to poor conclusions.

## Q52. What is data integration?

**Answer:** Data integration combines data from different systems or
formats into a consistent view. It can be difficult because sources may
use different schemas, identifiers, formats, and quality standards.

## Q53. How can Big Data storage costs be reduced?

**Answer:** Organizations can store data on scalable distributed
infrastructure, compress data, remove unnecessary duplicates, use
appropriate storage tiers, retain data according to its value and
requirements, and avoid processing data that is not needed.

## Q54. What is the complexity of Big Data analysis?

**Answer:** Data may be extremely large, arrive quickly, and use many
formats. Processing it may require distributed systems, specialized
algorithms, data cleaning, and skilled professionals.

## Q55. How can a startup manage Big Data costs?

**Answer:** Start with a clear business objective, collect only useful
data, use open-source tools where suitable, scale resources as demand
grows, optimize queries and jobs, compress or archive older data, and
monitor infrastructure spending.

## Q56. What problems might a healthcare provider face when adopting Big Data?

**Answer:** It may face privacy and security risks, inconsistent
records, integration problems across systems, high storage and
processing costs, difficulties interpreting complex data, and a shortage
of skilled staff. Controls, standardization, validation, scalable
infrastructure, and training can help.

## Q58. Why can a traditional system struggle with Big Data?

**Answer:** A single machine or conventional database may encounter
storage, memory, processing, integration, or scaling limits as data
volume and complexity increase. Distributed storage and parallel
processing can help handle these workloads.

## Q59. What is a bottleneck?

**Answer:** A bottleneck is a component that limits the throughput of
the overall system. In the restaurant analogy in the notes, adding more
cooks does not improve output if the shared food shelf cannot supply
them quickly enough.

## Q60. What does the restaurant analogy teach about distributed processing?

**Answer:** More workers can perform tasks in parallel, but shared
resources can become bottlenecks. A good distributed design must
distribute both work and data effectively rather than simply adding
processing units.

## Q61. What is horizontal scaling?

**Answer:** Horizontal scaling means increasing capacity by adding more
machines or nodes to a cluster.

## Q62. What is vertical scaling?

**Answer:** Vertical scaling means increasing the capacity of a single
machine, for example by adding CPU, RAM, or storage. Horizontal scaling
is central to Hadoop's distributed approach.

# 6. Introduction to Hadoop

## Q63. What is Hadoop?

**Answer:** Apache Hadoop is an open-source framework for storing and
processing large datasets across multiple computers. It distributes
storage and computation across a cluster rather than depending only on
one machine.

## Q64. Why do we need Hadoop?

**Answer:** Traditional systems may face limited storage capacity, slow
processing, poor scalability, and high costs when scaling a single
machine. Hadoop addresses many of these problems through distributed
storage, parallel processing, and horizontal scaling.

## Q65. List the features of Hadoop.

**Answer:** - Open-source. - Handles large datasets. - Supports
structured, semi-structured, and unstructured data. - Provides
distributed storage. - Supports parallel processing. - Scales by adding
nodes. - Provides fault tolerance through data replication and recovery
mechanisms.

## Q66. Explain Hadoop's principles.

**Answer:** 1. **Scalability:** Add or remove nodes as needs change. 2.
**Fault tolerance:** Recover from node or hardware failures. 3.
**Economical:** Open-source software can run on commodity hardware. 4.
**Large, flexible storage:** Store large and varied datasets. 5.
**Hardware-failure handling:** Replication helps preserve availability
of data. 6. **High availability:** Appropriate configurations can reduce
service interruption. 7. **Distributed storage:** Data is divided into
blocks and distributed. 8. **Distributed processing:** Tasks are split
and processed across nodes; MapReduce uses map and reduce phases.

## Q67. What is a cluster?

**Answer:** A cluster is a group of interconnected computers (nodes)
that cooperate to store data or execute processing tasks.

## Q68. What is a node?

**Answer:** A node is an individual computer or server participating in
the cluster. Depending on its role, it may manage metadata, store data
blocks, or execute computation.

## Q69. What is commodity hardware?

**Answer:** Commodity hardware refers to relatively standard, widely
available servers rather than relying exclusively on specialized,
expensive machines. Hadoop was designed to scale across such machines.

## Q70. What is fault tolerance?

**Answer:** Fault tolerance is a system's ability to continue operating
or recover when a component fails. HDFS replication and task
re-execution are examples of mechanisms that help Hadoop handle
failures.

## Q71. What is parallel processing?

**Answer:** Parallel processing divides a task into smaller parts that
can be processed simultaneously by multiple workers or nodes. It can
reduce elapsed processing time when the task and infrastructure support
parallel execution.

## Q72. List advantages of Hadoop.

**Answer:** Efficient processing of large datasets, parallel execution,
cost-effectiveness, horizontal scalability, distributed storage, and
fault tolerance.

## Q73. List applications of Hadoop from the notes.

**Answer:** Product and movie recommendations, banking fraud analysis,
hospital patient-data analysis, social-media trend analysis, and weather
or climate-data analysis.

## Q74. Is Hadoop always the best choice for real-time processing?

**Answer:** No. Traditional Hadoop MapReduce is mainly batch-oriented
and can have high latency. Real-time or low-latency workloads may need
technologies such as Kafka for ingestion and Spark or streaming engines
for processing.

# 7. Hadoop Architecture and Core Components

## Q75. Name the four core Hadoop components.

**Answer:** 1. **Hadoop Common** 2. **HDFS (Hadoop Distributed File
System)** 3. **Hadoop MapReduce** 4. **YARN (Yet Another Resource
Negotiator)**

## Q76. What is Hadoop Common?

**Answer:** Hadoop Common provides shared libraries, utilities, and
abstractions needed by the other Hadoop modules.

## Q77. What is HDFS?

**Answer:** HDFS is Hadoop's distributed file system. It stores large
files by dividing them into blocks and distributing those blocks across
DataNodes.

## Q78. What is MapReduce?

**Answer:** MapReduce is a programming model and processing framework
for distributed computation. The map phase transforms input records into
intermediate key-value pairs, and the reduce phase aggregates values
associated with the same key.

## Q79. What is YARN?

**Answer:** YARN manages cluster resources and schedules applications.
It allocates resources such as CPU and memory and coordinates the
execution of jobs.

## Q81. Explain the master-worker idea shown in the architecture diagram.

**Answer:** In the simplified architecture diagram, a master-side
service coordinates metadata or scheduling while worker nodes store
blocks and/or execute tasks. In HDFS, the NameNode manages metadata and
DataNodes store blocks. In YARN, resource-management responsibilities
are handled by YARN services. These roles should not be assumed to be
one single master process in every Hadoop deployment.

## Q82. Why is data locality useful?

**Answer:** Data locality means scheduling computation close to where
the required data is stored when possible. It can reduce network
transfer and improve processing efficiency.

# 8. HDFS Architecture

## Q83. What is HDFS?

**Answer:** HDFS stands for Hadoop Distributed File System. It is the
storage component of Hadoop and is designed to store large files across
multiple machines with fault tolerance.

## Q84. What are the main HDFS components?

**Answer:** 1. NameNode. 2. DataNodes. 3. HDFS client.

## Q85. What is the NameNode?

**Answer:** The NameNode is the master metadata service in HDFS. It
manages the filesystem namespace and tracks file-to-block mappings and
block locations. It does **not** normally store the actual file
contents.

## Q86. List the responsibilities of the NameNode.

**Answer:** - Maintains file names and directory structure. - Maintains
filesystem metadata and permissions. - Tracks which blocks make up a
file. - Tracks where block replicas are located. - Coordinates metadata
operations for file access.

## Q87. What is a DataNode?

**Answer:** A DataNode is a worker service that stores the actual HDFS
data blocks on local disks and serves read/write requests under the
NameNode's coordination.

## Q88. List the responsibilities of a DataNode.

**Answer:** - Stores data blocks. - Serves block data to clients. -
Sends heartbeats and block reports to the NameNode. - Participates in
block replication and recovery operations.

## Q89. What is a heartbeat?

**Answer:** A heartbeat is a periodic signal from a DataNode indicating
that it is alive and functioning. If the NameNode stops receiving
heartbeats for a configured period, it may treat the node as
unavailable.

## Q90. What is a block report?

**Answer:** A block report is information a DataNode sends to the
NameNode about the blocks it stores. It helps the NameNode track block
locations and replication state.

## Q91. What is the role of the HDFS client?

**Answer:** The client requests file operations, contacts the NameNode
for metadata and block locations, and transfers file data directly to or
from DataNodes.

## Q92. Does the NameNode store the actual file data?

**Answer:** No. It manages metadata. The actual file blocks are stored
on DataNodes.

## Q93. Explain HDFS file writing step by step.

**Answer:** 1. The client requests to create a file. 2. The NameNode
checks permissions and whether the operation is allowed. 3. The client
divides the file into blocks according to HDFS configuration. 4. The
NameNode selects suitable DataNodes for block replicas. 5. The client
streams block data to the selected DataNodes, usually through a
replication pipeline. 6. Acknowledgements are returned as data is
written. 7. Metadata is updated so the file and its blocks can be
located.

The notes give **128 MB** as a typical block size in modern Hadoop.
Actual block size is configurable.

## Q94. Explain HDFS file reading step by step.

**Answer:** 1. The client requests a file. 2. The NameNode returns
metadata and block locations. 3. The client reads the blocks directly
from appropriate DataNodes. 4. It reads the blocks in file order and
reconstructs the original file stream.

## Q95. Why does HDFS divide files into blocks?

**Answer:** Blocks allow large files to be distributed across nodes,
enable parallel access, and support replication for fault tolerance.

## Q96. What is replication in HDFS?

**Answer:** Replication means storing multiple copies of a block on
different DataNodes. If one node fails, another replica may be used. The
replication factor determines the configured number of copies.

## Q97. What is the default replication factor?

**Answer:** In many standard Hadoop configurations, the default
replication factor is **3**, but it is configurable. The supplied notes
explain replication without specifying a universal factor.

## Q98. What happens if a DataNode fails?

**Answer:** The NameNode detects that the DataNode is unavailable
through missing heartbeats. If blocks become under-replicated, HDFS can
arrange new replicas from surviving copies, subject to cluster health
and configuration.

## Q99. What happens if the NameNode fails?

**Answer:** In a simple single-NameNode setup, filesystem metadata
operations may become unavailable until the NameNode is recovered.
High-availability configurations use active/standby NameNodes and
supporting mechanisms to reduce this risk.

## Q100. Does HDFS suit millions of tiny files?

**Answer:** Not ideally. Each file and block requires metadata, which
consumes NameNode memory. A very large number of small files can create
metadata overhead and reduce efficiency. Combining small files or using
a more suitable storage format can help.

## Q101. What does "write once, read many times" mean in HDFS?

**Answer:** It describes a common HDFS workload pattern: files are
written and then read repeatedly for analysis, rather than being updated
in place frequently. HDFS supports append and other operations, but it
is not designed like a conventional database for frequent random
updates.

## Q102. What block size should you say in the viva?

**Answer:** According to the notes, a typical modern Hadoop HDFS block
size is **128 MB**. Mention that it is configurable, not a fixed rule
for every cluster.

# 9. Hadoop Ecosystem

## Q103. What is the Hadoop ecosystem?

**Answer:** The Hadoop ecosystem is a collection of tools and frameworks
that work together to ingest, store, process, query, coordinate,
monitor, and analyze Big Data.

## Q104. Name the four ecosystem layers in the notes.

**Answer:** 1. Data Storage. 2. Data Processing. 3. Data Access. 4. Data
Management.

## Q105. Explain the data storage layer.

**Answer:** This layer stores large amounts of data in a distributed
environment. - **HDFS:** Distributed file storage using blocks and
DataNodes. - **HBase:** A distributed column-family NoSQL database built
to work with HDFS and support random, near-real-time reads/writes.

## Q106. What is HBase?

**Answer:** HBase is a distributed NoSQL database modeled around column
families. It is designed for large tables and fast random access to
rows, unlike HDFS, which is primarily a distributed file system.

## Q107. Differentiate HDFS and HBase.

**Answer:** HDFS stores files as blocks and is well suited to large
sequential files and batch workloads. HBase stores data in tables and
supports low-latency random reads and writes by row key. HBase commonly
uses HDFS for its underlying storage.

## Q108. Explain the data processing layer.

**Answer:** - **MapReduce:** Processes large datasets using map and
reduce stages. - **YARN:** Manages cluster resources and schedules jobs.

## Q109. What is Hive?

**Answer:** Hive is a data warehouse system that enables querying and
analysis of data using a SQL-like language called **HiveQL**. It is
useful for batch analytics, summarization, and reporting.

## Q110. What is Pig?

**Answer:** Apache Pig is a high-level data-processing platform that
uses a scripting language called **Pig Latin** to express data
transformations and analysis workflows.

## Q111. Differentiate Hive and Pig.

**Answer:** Hive provides a SQL-like query language and is often used by
analysts familiar with SQL. Pig uses a procedural data-flow scripting
style and is useful for defining sequences of data transformations.

## Q112. What is Apache Mahout?

**Answer:** Mahout is a machine-learning library/project associated with
scalable algorithms such as clustering and recommendation techniques.
The notes list clustering, classification, and recommendations as
example uses.

## Q113. What is Avro?

**Answer:** Avro is a data serialization system that supports compact
data representation and schema-based data exchange between applications.

## Q114. What is Sqoop?

**Answer:** Sqoop was a tool for bulk data transfer between relational
databases and Hadoop systems. For example, it could import records from
MySQL into Hadoop storage or export results back to a database.
*Modern-version note:* Apache Sqoop is now retired, but it remains
relevant to older Hadoop syllabi.

## Q115. What is Oozie?

**Answer:** Oozie is a workflow scheduler historically used to
coordinate and schedule Hadoop jobs, such as running a processing
pipeline every night.

## Q116. What is Chukwa?

**Answer:** Chukwa is a data collection and monitoring system described
in the notes as collecting logs and performance information from
distributed systems.

## Q117. What is Flume?

**Answer:** Apache Flume is a distributed service for collecting,
aggregating, and moving event data such as server logs into storage
systems such as HDFS.

## Q118. What is ZooKeeper?

**Answer:** ZooKeeper is a coordination service used by distributed
applications for configuration, synchronization, naming, and
coordination between services.

## Q119. Differentiate Flume and Sqoop.

**Answer:** Flume is associated with collecting and moving
event/streaming log data. Sqoop was associated with bulk transfer
between relational databases and Hadoop.

## Q120. Differentiate Oozie and ZooKeeper.

**Answer:** Oozie schedules and coordinates workflows or jobs. ZooKeeper
provides coordination primitives and shared configuration services for
distributed applications.

## Q121. Place these tools into the four layers from the notes.

**Answer:** - **Storage:** HDFS, HBase. - **Processing:** MapReduce,
YARN. - **Access:** Hive, Pig, Mahout, Avro, Sqoop. - **Management:**
Oozie, Chukwa, Flume, ZooKeeper.

*Note:* Ecosystem-layer classifications can vary by diagram or textbook;
use the classification given in your course notes for the viva.

# 10. MapReduce Algorithm and Worked Example

## Q122. What is MapReduce?

**Answer:** MapReduce is a distributed programming model for processing
large datasets. It commonly has a map stage that emits intermediate
key-value pairs and a reduce stage that aggregates values by key.

## Q123. What are the main stages of MapReduce?

**Answer:** 1. Input splitting and record reading. 2. **Map:** Convert
each input record into intermediate key-value pairs. 3. Shuffle and
sort: Group intermediate values by key. 4. **Reduce:** Aggregate values
for each key. 5. Write output.

## Q124. Explain the word-count algorithm.

**Problem:** Count the number of occurrences of each word in a document.

**Algorithm / pseudocode:**

``` text
WORDCOUNT-MAP(document):
    for each line in document:
        for each word in line:
            emit(word, 1)

WORDCOUNT-REDUCE(word, values):
    total = 0
    for each value in values:
        total = total + value
    emit(word, total)
```

## Q125. Work out a word-count example.

**Input:**

``` text
big data big
hadoop data
```

**Map output:**

``` text
(big, 1)
(data, 1)
(big, 1)
(hadoop, 1)
(data, 1)
```

**Shuffle/group by key:**

``` text
big    -> [1, 1]
data   -> [1, 1]
hadoop -> [1]
```

**Reduce output:**

``` text
big    -> 2
data   -> 2
hadoop -> 1
```

**Final answer:** `big: 2`, `data: 2`, `hadoop: 1`.

## Q126. What is a key-value pair?

**Answer:** It is a pair consisting of a key used to identify or group a
record and a value containing associated information. For word count,
the map emits `(word, 1)`.

## Q127. What is shuffle and sort?

**Answer:** It is the stage that transfers intermediate map output to
reducers, groups records with the same key, and sorts keys as required
by the framework. It prepares the data for reduction.

## Q128. Why is Reduce needed?

**Answer:** Reduce combines or aggregates all values associated with the
same key. In word count, it adds the ones for each word to produce a
total frequency.

## Q129. Is MapReduce the same as Hadoop?

**Answer:** No. Hadoop is the broader framework/ecosystem. MapReduce is
one computation model used within Hadoop. HDFS handles storage, YARN
manages resources, and Hadoop Common supplies shared utilities.

## Q130. What is the time complexity of word count?

**Answer:** For a simple local implementation that scans all characters
or tokens, time is typically **O(N)** in the input size, assuming token
processing is linear. In distributed MapReduce, total work remains
proportional to input size in a simple count, but actual elapsed time
depends on data partitioning, shuffle cost, number of workers, I/O, and
skew. Do not claim a single universal wall-clock complexity for a Hadoop
cluster.

## Q131. Does MapReduce process data in real time?

**Answer:** Standard Hadoop MapReduce is primarily batch-oriented. It is
effective for large-scale offline processing but is not ideal for
millisecond-latency streaming decisions without additional systems.

# 11. Commands and Practical Viva

The supplied module notes focus mainly on concepts and architecture
rather than a full command reference. The following are useful practical
commands commonly associated with a Hadoop lab.

## Q132. How do you check whether Hadoop daemons are running?

**Answer:**

``` bash
jps
```

It lists Java processes. Depending on the configuration and running
services, Hadoop-related processes may include NameNode, DataNode,
ResourceManager, and NodeManager.

## Q133. How do you list files in the HDFS root directory?

**Answer:**

``` bash
hdfs dfs -ls /
```

## Q134. How do you create an HDFS directory?

**Answer:**

``` bash
hdfs dfs -mkdir -p /user/hadoop/input
```

## Q135. How do you copy a local file to HDFS?

**Answer:**

``` bash
hdfs dfs -put data.txt /user/hadoop/input/
```

The `-copyFromLocal` command is another common option.

## Q136. How do you display a file stored in HDFS?

**Answer:**

``` bash
hdfs dfs -cat /user/hadoop/input/data.txt
```

## Q137. How do you copy a file from HDFS to the local file system?

**Answer:**

``` bash
hdfs dfs -get /user/hadoop/input/data.txt .
```

## Q138. How do you remove a file from HDFS?

**Answer:**

``` bash
hdfs dfs -rm /user/hadoop/input/data.txt
```

## Q139. What is the difference between `-put` and `-get`?

**Answer:** `-put` transfers a local file into HDFS. `-get` transfers a
file from HDFS to the local file system.

## Q140. What is the difference between `hdfs dfs` and `hadoop fs`?

**Answer:** Both are used to run filesystem shell operations. `hdfs dfs`
is HDFS-specific in intent, while `hadoop fs` is the more general
filesystem-shell form. Exact behavior depends on the Hadoop version and
filesystem configuration.

# 12. Case Study: RetailMax

## Q141. Summarize the RetailMax case study.

**Answer:** RetailMax is described as an e-commerce and logistics
company processing transactions, shipments, and user behavior. It
adopted HDFS, MapReduce, Hive, and Pig to manage and analyze terabytes
of data daily.

## Q142. What limitations might RetailMax experience?

**Answer:** - Batch processing may not support instant
recommendations. - MapReduce job startup and disk I/O can add latency. -
A large number of small files can create metadata overhead. -
Integrating multiple data sources can be complex. - Cluster management
and resource tuning require skills. - Real-time anomaly detection may
need additional streaming tools.

## Q143. How could RetailMax improve its architecture?

**Answer:** Keep HDFS and batch processing for historical analysis where
appropriate; use a streaming ingestion layer for events; use a
low-latency processing engine for real-time needs; optimize file formats
and partitioning; monitor jobs and resource use; and apply governance
and access controls.

# 13. Case Study: FinTrack Solutions

## Q144. What is the FinTrack problem?

**Answer:** FinTrack initially used Hadoop for cost-effective, scalable
analysis of historical financial transactions. As the company grew, it
needed real-time fraud detection, continuous user monitoring,
low-latency alerts, and support for streaming mobile/web transactions.

## Q145. Why was traditional MapReduce unsuitable for the new requirement?

**Answer:** MapReduce is batch-oriented and can take minutes or longer
for a job to complete. Fraud alerts may need to be generated in
milliseconds or seconds, so batch processing alone cannot meet the
latency requirement.

## Q146. What is the small-files problem?

**Answer:** HDFS metadata for every file and block consumes NameNode
memory. Millions of tiny files can increase metadata overhead and make
storage and processing inefficient.

## Q147. Why can iterative machine learning be inefficient in MapReduce?

**Answer:** Some machine-learning algorithms repeatedly update a model
over multiple iterations. MapReduce jobs often write intermediate data
to disk and have job-startup overhead, making repeated iterations slower
and more cumbersome than engines designed for iterative processing.

## Q148. Does native Hadoop MapReduce provide a complete streaming-processing solution?

**Answer:** No. Hadoop MapReduce is mainly for batch processing.
Streaming systems or engines such as Apache Kafka for event ingestion
and Apache Spark's streaming capabilities may be integrated to support
low-latency workloads.

## Q149. What migration outcome is described in the notes?

**Answer:** The notes state that FinTrack migrated to: - **Apache
Kafka** for data ingestion. - **Apache Spark** for low-latency
processing and machine-learning deployment. - **AWS** for scalable
storage and processing.

## Q150. Differentiate Kafka and Spark in this case.

**Answer:** Kafka is primarily an event-streaming platform used to
publish, store, and distribute streams of records. Spark is a
distributed processing engine that can process batch and streaming data
and run analytics or machine-learning workloads. They complement rather
than replace one another.

# 14. Tricky External-Viva Questions

## Q151. If Hadoop is fault-tolerant, can data never be lost?

**Answer:** No system can guarantee that under every possible failure.
Replication reduces risk, but multiple failures, misconfiguration,
accidental deletion, or other problems can still cause loss. Backups,
replication policies, and recovery planning remain important.

## Q152. If HDFS stores three copies, does it triple the usable storage?

**Answer:** Replication consumes additional physical storage. With a
replication factor of three, storing one logical block typically
requires space for three replicas, so the usable capacity for replicated
data is lower than raw disk capacity.

## Q153. Does the NameNode send all file data to the client?

**Answer:** No. The NameNode provides metadata and block locations. The
client transfers actual file data directly to and from DataNodes.

## Q154. Why not store all Big Data in an RDBMS?

**Answer:** An RDBMS can be suitable for many workloads, but a single
conventional database may not be economical or practical for some
extremely large, varied, distributed analytical workloads. The correct
choice depends on data volume, latency, consistency, query needs, and
operational constraints.

## Q155. Is every large dataset automatically Big Data?

**Answer:** Not necessarily. Big Data is relative to the tools and
infrastructure available and is often characterized by a combination of
scale, speed, variety, and processing challenges rather than a universal
size threshold.

## Q156. What is the difference between scalability and fault tolerance?

**Answer:** Scalability is the ability to handle increasing workloads by
adding resources. Fault tolerance is the ability to continue operating
or recover when components fail.

## Q157. What is the difference between high availability and fault tolerance?

**Answer:** High availability aims to minimize downtime and keep a
service accessible. Fault tolerance focuses on continuing operation or
recovering correctly despite failures. They overlap but are not
identical.

## Q158. Why does adding more nodes not always make a job faster?

**Answer:** Performance can be limited by network bandwidth, disk I/O,
task overhead, data skew, coordination, or a shared bottleneck. Some
workloads also cannot be parallelized effectively.

## Q159. What is data locality, and why does it matter?

**Answer:** Data locality means running computation near the data when
possible. It reduces network traffic and can improve performance.

## Q160. Can Hadoop store unstructured data?

**Answer:** Yes. HDFS can store files in many formats, including text,
images, audio, and video. Storing a file does not automatically mean
Hadoop understands its contents; additional tools are needed to analyze
it.

## Q161. Which is used for SQL-like queries: Hive or Pig?

**Answer:** Hive uses HiveQL, which is SQL-like. Pig uses Pig Latin.

## Q162. Which is used for distributed file storage: HDFS or MapReduce?

**Answer:** HDFS is used for distributed storage. MapReduce is used for
distributed computation.

## Q163. Which component schedules resources: YARN or HDFS?

**Answer:** YARN manages resources and schedules applications. HDFS
manages distributed file storage.

## Q164. What happens if a file is larger than one HDFS block?

**Answer:** It is split into multiple blocks. The blocks can be stored
on different DataNodes, and the client reads them in the correct order
to reconstruct the file.

## Q165. Why is Big Data quality important even if the dataset is huge?

**Answer:** More data does not automatically mean better results.
Inaccurate, duplicated, incomplete, or biased data can produce
misleading analysis and poor decisions.
